Cleaning quote review checklist

Use this before you send a residential cleaning estimate. It is designed to catch missing scope and cost assumptions—not to replace an inspection or your judgment.

Check the numbers

Property and access

  • Service address and service date are confirmed.
  • Home size, bedrooms, bathrooms, and occupied/vacant status are confirmed.
  • Property condition and last professional clean are understood.
  • Parking, stairs, elevator, gate, and entry instructions are accounted for.
  • Pets, allergies, delicate surfaces, or product restrictions are noted.

Scope

  • Standard tasks are written clearly.
  • Oven, refrigerator, interior windows, cabinets, laundry, and organization are included or excluded explicitly.
  • Heavy buildup, pet hair, post-construction dust, or other unusual conditions are priced separately.
  • Client-supplied versus cleaner-supplied products are confirmed.
  • Recurring-service frequency and first-clean differences are clear.

Numbers

  • Total team labor hours—not only clock time—are estimated.
  • Labor target reflects your real business situation.
  • Supplies, travel, parking, disposal, and job-specific costs are included.
  • Overhead and a deliberate profit buffer are included.
  • The final number meets your minimum viable job price.

Client expectations

  • Quote validity period and payment timing are stated.
  • Cancellation, rescheduling, and access expectations are clear.
  • The client knows what could change the estimate.
  • The quote and scope have been reviewed for errors before sending.
Keep learning: after completion, record estimated versus actual team hours and unexpected costs. Use that history to improve the next quote.
